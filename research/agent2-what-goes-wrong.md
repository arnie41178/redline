# Agent 2: What Goes Wrong — Ranked Clause Types by Frequency/Severity

**Research date:** 2026-11-07
**Search budget used:** 7 searches, 5 pages read
**Sourced findings:** 9

---

## Methodology

Searched FTC enforcement data, CFPB complaints, legal analysis sites, and practitioner-facing sources. Ranked by a combination of: (a) documented complaint volume, (b) regulatory enforcement attention, and (c) financial severity of harm. Where data was quantitative, it is cited directly. Where sources were qualitative/practitioner, they are noted as such.

---

## Ranked Findings

---

### #1 — Auto-Renewal / Evergreen Clauses

**Rank rationale:** Highest documented complaint volume of any single clause type, backed by FTC enforcement data with over 100,000 complaints in five years and $2.5B+ in settlements.

**How it burns people:**
Subscription auto-renewal clauses bury renewal terms in fine print, charge consumers without clear notice, and design cancellation flows to be intentionally obstructive. The FTC documented that Uber One required users to navigate "up to 23 screens and 32 actions" to cancel.

**Frequency data:**
- FTC received **90+ negative-option/auto-renewal complaints per day** in 2025, up from ~33/day in 2020 — nearly a 3x increase in five years
- Total complaints exceeded **100,000** over the past five years
- **13 major enforcement cases** between June 2024 and June 2026
- Amazon Prime settlement alone: **$2.5 billion** ($1B civil penalty + $1.5B consumer redress) covering ~35 million consumers
- Other settlements (2024–2026): Instacart $60M, Shutterstock $35M, Cleo AI $17M, Match Group $14M, Care.com $8.5M, Chegg $7.5M

**Representative example:**
Cleo AI advertised "instant cash advances" but required consumers to enroll in auto-renewing monthly subscriptions at $5.99–$14.99/month. FTC settlement: $17 million (March 2025).

**Sources:**
- https://clauseaudit.com/data/ftc-subscription-enforcement-tracker
- https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/04/ftc-signals-enforcement-on-auto-renewing-subscriptions-and-sales
- https://newmedialaw.proskauer.com/2026/07/24/ftcs-shutterstock-settlement-signals-continued-scrutiny-of-auto-renewal-practices/

### #2 — Mandatory Arbitration / Class-Action Waivers

**Rank rationale:** Second-highest documented complaint volume at the CFPB, plus massive scale — hundreds of millions of Americans are covered by these clauses. The CFPB logged 2,393 complaints referencing arbitration in 2025 alone (a 9% year-over-year increase), and the CFPB received nearly **5.6 million total complaints** in 2025.

**How it burns people:**
Arbitration clauses bar consumers and workers from going to court and block class actions, leaving individual claimants to navigate a costly private process for small-dollar disputes that are uneconomical to pursue individually. Three-quarters of consumers don't know their credit card agreement includes one. Among 57 million Sprint customers, only 6 sought arbitration in four years — not because there were no disputes, but because individual arbitration is effectively inaccessible.

**Frequency data:**
- Estimated **60+ million workers** in the US are covered by mandatory arbitration agreements (EPI data via search results)
- Fewer than **7% of consumers** are aware their contract bars them from suing in court
- The CFPB's proposed January 2025 rule would have banned these clauses in consumer financial contracts; it was withdrawn but reflected documented harm scale

**Representative quote:**
"Forced arbitration clauses bar harmed consumers from going before a court, and pre-dispute dispute resolution clauses add extra hurdles making it more difficult for consumers to go to arbitration." — CFPB, 2025

**Sources:**
- https://www.americanprogress.org/article/the-case-against-mandatory-consumer-arbitration-clauses/
- https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/01/14/2025-00633/prohibited-terms-and-conditions-in-agreements-for-consumer-financial-products-or-services-regulation
- https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2024/2024-06-11-cfpb-warns-against-deceptive-terms-in-consumer-contracts

### #3 — Security Deposit Terms (Lease Contracts)

**Rank rationale:** Security deposit disputes are the #2 most common landlord-tenant conflict category and the most common type of small claims case filed by tenants in the US.

**How it burns people:**
Lease clauses on security deposits typically lack specificity about what qualifies as "normal wear and tear" vs. deductible damage, give landlords wide discretion on deductions, and impose vague return timelines. This generates disputes on nearly every residential tenancy exit.

**Frequency data:**
- Security deposit disputes rank **#2 among all tenant-landlord conflicts**, behind only rent payment disputes
- **21% of renters** report having experienced a legal conflict with their landlord
- Security deposit cases account for a "large percentage" of small claims filings nationally (Nolo)
- The most expensive disputes center on "normal wear and tear" — a term present in nearly every residential lease but rarely defined

**Representative example:**
The most contentious clause pattern: a lease defines the tenant's responsibility as returning the unit in "the same condition as received, normal wear and tear excepted" — but defines neither what qualifies as normal wear nor the documentation standard, giving landlords full discretion to withhold deposits.

**Sources:**
- https://managecasa.com/articles/top-10-common-disputes-between-tenants-and-rental-owners
- https://www.nolo.com/legal-encyclopedia/security-deposit-disputes
- https://shouldisignthislease.com/clauses/security-deposit/

### #4 — Unilateral Termination (Without Kill Fee / Notice)

**Rank rationale:** Identified as "High Risk" by freelance contract analysis sources; affects all freelancers and service contractors. Direct financial harm (unpaid hours, schedule gaps) is immediate and common.

**How it burns people:**
A unilateral termination clause lets a client end a contract "at any time, for any reason, with immediate effect and without further obligation." This transfers 100% of project risk to the contractor: completed-but-unpaid work, materials ordered, schedule gaps, and subcontractor commitments all become the contractor's loss.

**Frequency data:**
- Identified as the #5 red flag in a 2026 analysis of freelance contract clauses (clauseguard.io)
- No aggregate complaint statistics found for this specific clause type; harm is documented through practitioner sources and case patterns

**Representative clause (verbatim):**
"Client may terminate this agreement at any time, for any reason, with immediate effect and without further obligation to Contractor."

**Representative example:**
A freelance developer builds out a feature for 3 weeks; client kills the project the day before delivery. Without a kill fee clause, the contractor has no recourse for the completed work.

**Sources:**
- https://clauseguard.io/blog/7-contract-red-flags-freelancers-2026

### #5 — Broad IP Assignment (Freelance / Work-for-Hire Contracts)

**Rank rationale:** Identified as "High Risk" by multiple practitioner sources. The specific harm — losing ownership of pre-existing tools and frameworks retroactively — is well-documented with case examples and legal costs.

**How it burns people:**
Broad IP assignment clauses (e.g., "all intellectual property created, developed, or **utilized** in connection with the Services") go beyond project deliverables to capture pre-existing tools, templates, code libraries, and design systems. A single word like "utilized" can hand ownership of independently developed assets to one client.

**Frequency data:**
- No aggregate statistics found; documented through practitioner analysis and case examples

**Representative examples:**
1. A graphic designer with a full IP assignment and no portfolio license received a cease-and-desist from an acquiring company when she featured the work in her own portfolio.
2. A software developer's client claimed ownership of a 2-year-old proprietary testing framework used on the project. Resolution cost: **$15,000 in legal fees**.

**Sources:**
- https://clauseguard.io/blog/7-contract-red-flags-freelancers-2026
- https://flag.red/contract-guides/ip-ownership-in-freelance-agreement
- https://aaronhall.com/ip-assignment-failures-freelance-branding/

### #6 — Non-Compete Clauses

**Rank rationale:** Subject of federal regulatory action (FTC attempted blanket ban in 2024, blocked by courts), with ongoing targeted enforcement against overbroad use. Scale of workers affected is very large.

**How it burns people:**
Non-competes prevent workers from taking jobs or clients in their field after leaving an employer, often for 12–24 months across a broad geography. Overbroad clauses (e.g., applied to low-wage workers with no access to trade secrets) generate legal costs even when the clause is ultimately unenforceable. For freelancers, industry-wide non-competes can block work across entire sectors.

**Frequency data:**
- The FTC's proposed 2024 rule — which would have banned most non-competes — was blocked by a federal court in August 2024
- FTC filed enforcement actions in September 2025 (pet cremation company) and December 2025 (Adamas Amenity Services) for overbroad non-competes on low-wage workers
- Freelance non-competes rated "Medium Risk" but generate legal fees even when unenforceable

**Representative example:**
FTC complaint against a pet cremation company for requiring non-competes on "almost all employees regardless of skill level or job duties."

**Sources:**
- https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/260429-ftc-noncompete-enforcement
- https://beancount.io/blog/2026/07/09/ftc-noncompete-ban-withdrawn-small-employer-enforcement-guide
- https://clauseguard.io/blog/7-contract-red-flags-freelancers-2026

### #7 — Indemnification / One-Sided Indemnity

**Rank rationale:** Identified as a high-risk clause for small businesses and freelancers. The specific danger — that indemnification is often carved out of liability caps — is structural and poorly understood by most signers.

**How it burns people:**
Broad indemnification clauses shift liability for the other party's mistakes to the signer. The critical structural trap: a contract may include a $50,000 liability cap while a separate paragraph says "the limitation of liability shall not apply to indemnification obligations" — functionally making indemnity exposure unlimited despite the cap.

**Frequency data:**
- No aggregate statistics found; documented through legal analysis sources
- Standard general liability insurance frequently does NOT cover all indemnification obligations, creating a hidden coverage gap

**Representative clause (verbatim):**
"Contractor shall indemnify, defend, and hold harmless Client from any claims, damages, or expenses arising from or related to the Services provided under this agreement."

**Sources:**
- https://mhslaw.com/the-hidden-danger-in-your-contracts-indemnification-clauses-that-can-sink-a-small-business/
- https://www.gleamlaw.com/blog/commercial-litigation/indemnification-clauses-and-commercial-litigation-what-you-need-to-know-before-a-dispute-arises/
- https://www.margolispllc.com/post/limitation-of-liability

### #8 — Personal Guarantees (Commercial Leases)

**Rank rationale:** Specific to commercial leases; a clause that undoes the entire liability protection of business incorporation. Financial exposure can be catastrophic (3–5 years of rent + fees). Buried placement in contracts makes it systematically overlooked.

**How it burns people:**
A personal guarantee in a commercial lease makes the business owner personally liable if the business fails to pay rent, piercing the LLC/corporate liability shield. Landlords can pursue personal savings, assets, and future earnings. Some leases require spouses to co-sign, extending exposure into the household.

**Frequency data:**
- No aggregate statistics found
- Unlimited personal guarantees on 5-year NNN leases are described as standard practice in commercial real estate

**Representative example:**
A business owner signs a 5-year NNN lease with unlimited personal guarantee. Business closes after 2 years. Landlord pursues the owner for 36 months of remaining rent plus CAM charges, legal fees, and accelerated rent from the default clause.

**Sources:**
- https://www.joycegraddy.com/blog/2025/05/the-clause-that-turns-a-business-lease-into-a-personal-risk/
- https://leaselens.org/blog/personal-guarantee-explained
- https://theleasinglawyers.com/post/personal-guarantee-commercial-lease

### #9 — Unilateral Amendment Clauses

**Rank rationale:** Identified by CFPB as a distinct harmful clause type in consumer financial contracts. Allows companies to change contract terms without meaningful consumer consent.

**How it burns people:**
These clauses allow one party (typically the company/platform) to modify terms unilaterally, with notice buried in a mass email. Any modifications are structurally likely to benefit the company; the consumer has no option to reject the change short of terminating the relationship (which may itself trigger fees).

**Frequency data:**
- Specifically called out in the CFPB's proposed January 2025 rulemaking on prohibited contract terms
- No isolated complaint statistics found for this clause type specifically

**Representative quote:**
"Unilateral contract amendments can harm consumers since any modifications are likely to mainly benefit the company and the consumer has no option to reject the change." — CFPB analysis, 2025

**Sources:**
- https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/01/14/2025-00633/prohibited-terms-and-conditions-in-agreements-for-consumer-financial-products-or-services-regulation
- https://katten.com/cfpb-proposes-a-new-rule-prohibiting-certain-contractual-terms-for-consumer-financial-products

## What Was Not Found

- **Fee escalators / rent escalation clauses**: No aggregate complaint data or enforcement actions found within search budget. Exists as a known issue in commercial real estate but not surfaced in complaint datasets.
- **Early termination fees (ETF) for apartment leases**: Found regulatory attention (California 2025 proposed legislation) and individual complaint examples (e.g., $9,000 charged to a domestic violence survivor), but no aggregate complaint statistics.
- **Late fees**: No aggregate complaint data surfaced within search budget.
- **Liability caps specifically**: Addressed indirectly under indemnification (the interaction between caps and indemnity carve-outs); no standalone complaint data found.

---

## Notes on Data Quality

- Auto-renewal (#1) and arbitration (#2) have the strongest quantitative backing (government enforcement databases).
- Security deposit (#3) is backed by survey data and practitioner sources.
- Clauses #4–#9 are supported by practitioner analysis and representative case examples, but lack aggregate complaint statistics. They should be treated as qualitatively validated rather than statistically ranked against each other.
